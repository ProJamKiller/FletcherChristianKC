Israel is not “Israel”

I am not talking about Jews as a people. I am talking about a government, a war, an influence structure, and a political class in America that folds faster than a lawn chair at a tornado picnic whenever Israel is involved.

Israel is not “Israel”

There is a particular kind of brain rot that sets in when people quit reading a holy book and start wearing it like night-vision goggles over a missile silo. They stop wrestling with the text and start dry-humping it into public policy. They stop treating scripture like something ancient, wounded, layered, contradictory, and human, and start treating it like a celestial permission slip for whatever fresh hell the war machine coughed up this morning. That is exactly what is happening when American Christians talk about the modern State of Israel like it just dropped out of the sky in shrink-wrap straight from the Book of God. They take the Israel of Torah, prophets, exile, poetry, redaction, tribal memory, myth, bloodline obsession, priestly cleanup, and centuries of human collapse, then bolt it onto a modern nation-state with bombs, walls, surveillance, airstrikes, and lobbying networks, like some kind of Frankenstein gospel stitched together in a defense contractor’s basement. Then they tell the faithful this is holy. That questioning any of it is rebellion against God. That is not faith. That is spiritual moonshine cooked in a bunker and served to people who already mistake certainty for wisdom.

Because in the actual Hebrew Bible, “Israel” is not one neat, stainless-steel political unit marching through history with a flag in one hand and a deed in the other. It shifts. It fractures. It gets remembered differently depending on who is writing, when they are writing, and what catastrophe just tore through the landscape. Sometimes “Israel” means the covenant people. Sometimes it means the northern kingdom. Sometimes it means a remnant staring at the ruins, trying to stitch identity together with whatever scraps history forgot to burn. Even the twelve tribes do not function like some tidy divine spreadsheet. They look more like literary architecture built after collapse, a way of imposing order on a world that had already split at the seams. The tribe talk is not always a census. Sometimes it is a survival story wearing genealogical makeup. (1)

And that matters, because once people collapse biblical Israel into the modern State of Israel, morality gets dragged behind the truck and skinned alive on the highway. Then every policy becomes sacred by default. Every atrocity gets fogged over with incense and hallelujahs. Every apartment building turned into a concrete sandwich is treated like a mysterious chapter in God’s screenplay. Every dead child becomes an unfortunate but somehow necessary extra in an apocalyptic snuff film narrated by men in loafers who have never heard a missile whistle over their own roof. The modern State of Israel came into being in 1948 through war, partition, British withdrawal, displacement, diaspora return, and modern political force. (2) That is history. Modern, ugly, contested history. Not the magical return of a fully intact Bronze Age tribal confederation like some biblical boy band reunion with F-35s. Jewish connection to the land is real. Jewish history is real. Jewish trauma is real. None of that means a modern government gets to wrap itself in God like a stolen police badge and beat moral scrutiny to death in the alley.

And let’s stop pretending this is all theoretical. Gaza is not a seminar. Gaza is not a footnote. Gaza is not some moody little theological puzzle for Christian podcasters with ring lights and untreated authoritarian fantasies. Gaza is rubble, dust, starvation, amputation, panic, and families living inside the mouth of a machine that keeps chewing whether the cameras are on or not. The U.N.’s humanitarian report from March 19, 2026 warned again about the worsening civilian catastrophe and a pattern of unlawful killings after Palestinian families were reportedly killed by Israeli fire. (3) The International Court of Justice already said Israel’s continued presence in the occupied Palestinian territory is unlawful. (4) U.N. human rights experts called that finding historic for a reason. (5) That is not random activist graffiti spray-painted on a bathroom wall. That is international legal judgment hitting the table like a whiskey bottle at the end of a bad night. So when people act like criticizing Israeli state violence is antisemitism, they are either lying, panicking, or spiritually sedated. Criticizing a government is not hatred of a people. Opposing a state’s conduct is not hatred of an ethnicity. Refusing to baptize military violence because prophecy addicts told you it was holy does not make you a bigot. It makes you a human being with a pulse and at least one functioning moral nerve.

Then there is the American side of this circus, where the clown car is on fire and somehow still gaining speed. Joe Kent resigned this week as director of the National Counterterrorism Center and said publicly that Iran posed no imminent threat and that the war was pushed by pressure from Israel and its U.S. lobby. Reuters covered it. (6) The Associated Press covered it. (7) The Washington Post covered it. (8) Now, let me be crystal clear for the dim and dishonest people in the back who hear any criticism of state pressure and immediately start foaming about antisemitism: Jews are not responsible for the behavior of a government, and criticizing lobbying pressure or war advocacy is not a license to start slobbering out centuries-old ethnic garbage. I am not talking about Jews as a people. I am talking about a government, a war, an influence structure, and a political class in America that folds faster than a lawn chair at a tornado picnic whenever Israel is involved. Kent’s resignation matters because it punches a hole through the fairy-tale wallpaper. It tells you this war did not just fall from heaven like a flaming fax from Yahweh. It tells you there is a crack inside the national security state itself. And still, the evangelical machine keeps waving pom-poms like a meth choir at Armageddon rehearsal.

This is where the theology gets especially toxic, because American evangelical culture has spent decades marinating people in the belief that modern Israel must be defended not because of international law, not because of consistent ethics, not because of equal human dignity, but because a particular prophetic script demands it. That script is a theological slot machine for people who have mistaken mass death for divine suspense. Pull the lever. Watch the reels spin. Jerusalem, Iran, temple, heifer, war, antichrist, trumpet, blood. Maybe this time Jesus shows up. It is diseased. It is childish. It is one of the most dangerous spiritual pathologies on earth because it trains people to greet real human suffering like fans waiting for the next episode.

Thomas Jefferson understood the broad mechanism even if he never had to watch it wearing Fox News makeup and grinning through a fundraiser. Once religious certainty fuses with state power, dissent becomes heresy and violence starts dressing itself up like duty. That is the old scam. Crusades ran on it. State churches ran on it. Colonizers ran on it. Empires ran on it. You wrap conquest in sacred language, stuff domination into a hymn, and suddenly people will cheer for horrors they would otherwise recognize as horrors. That same scam is alive right now. Wrap a missile in prophecy. Wrap occupation in covenant. Wrap dead children in phrases like “God’s plan,” and watch millions of comfortable Christians surrender their conscience like it was just another coat at the door. (9)

So no, the modern State of Israel is not simply the same thing as the Israel of the Hebrew Bible, and pretending otherwise is not reverence. It is how scripture gets dragged into the targeting computer. It is how policy becomes insulated from moral thought. It is how apartment blocks collapse under verses. It is how dead children disappear beneath prophecy charts. It is how millions of Christians get conned into treating a modern state like an extension of God’s nervous system. And once that happens, you can sell them almost anything. War. Occupation. Ethnic domination. Regional escalation. All you have to do is wave a Bible over the smoke and tell them the fire is holy.

That is the sickness.

Not Judaism. Not Jewish existence. Not history.

The sickness is what happens when a state grabs a sacred text by the throat and says, you work for me now.

Nobody’s Mad at the Bible. They’re Mad at What You Do in Its Name.

I didn’t escape the Bible because I hated it. I escaped what it did to my sense of self—and what I watched it do to other people when it got tied to power.

Nobody’s Mad at the Bible. They’re Mad at What You Do in Its Name.

Nobody’s mad at the Bible.

That’s the first lie. The polished one. The safe one. The church-lobby version. The one printed on coffee mugs and fed to people like backlash just fell out of a clear blue sky for no reason. It lets believers pretend the anger is irrational instead of earned.

I didn’t walk away because I was too lazy to read it or too bitter to understand it. I knew the language. I knew the system. I studied the damn thing, taught it, lived inside it, built a whole identity around it. I know what it feels like when belief becomes architecture. When it stops being a set of ideas and becomes the walls in your head. That kind of immersion does not leave you untouched. It trains you to filter your thoughts, mistrust your instincts, and call that holiness.

That is what people who were never fully inside it do not always understand. This is not just about disagreement with a text. It is about what happens to a human being when they are taught, from the deepest place, that they are morally suspect before they have even had a chance to become themselves. You are told your heart is deceitful. Your flesh is weak. Your desires are dangerous. Your doubt is rebellion. Your suffering is either a test, a punishment, or proof that you still need more God. That is not just theology. That is a script for self-alienation.

And it does damage.

There is a growing body of clinical and academic literature around religious trauma, scrupulosity, shame, and self-blame. A 2024 review in Cureus described religious trauma as a real and measurable pattern of psychological harm, often rooted in fear-based teaching, chronic guilt, and authoritarian control. A 2025 study in the Journal of Humanistic Psychology found strong links between adverse religious experiences and later mental health strain, especially when religion was used to suppress autonomy and identity. These are not just internet buzzwords for people mad at church camp. This is a serious area of study, and it keeps pointing to the same ugly truth: when a faith system tells people they are broken by default and only safe through submission, it can leave psychic shrapnel behind. (1) (2)

And then that same system gets attached to political power and turns from personal torment into public menace.

That is where the mask comes off. It is not just that the Bible has been used to produce shame. It is that it has been used, over and over, to sanctify hierarchy, punish dissent, crush minorities, and dress raw domination up like righteousness. The language changes. The suits get more expensive. The stage lights get brighter. But the mechanism stays the same. You take ancient words, flatten them into certainty, strip out complexity, and point them like a weapon at whoever needs to be controlled.

We are watching that happen again in the United States right now. Christian nationalism is no longer some fringe fever dream muttered into a folding chair in the church basement. It is in school board meetings, campaign speeches, legislation, court rhetoric, and the bloodstream of a movement that wants to erase the line between church and state by pretending the line was immoral to begin with. Political scientists and legal scholars have warned that Christian nationalism is not simply private faith made public. It is a power project. It fuses religious identity with national identity and treats outsiders, dissenters, and pluralism itself as threats to be subdued. The Baptist Joint Committee, PRRI, and other groups tracking this have shown how closely Christian nationalist views correlate with hostility toward democratic norms, minority rights, and institutional restraint. (3) (4)

That is why people are not mad at the Bible in the abstract. They are mad at what is done in its name.

They are mad that women’s bodies get legislated by men quoting God like a zoning ordinance. They are mad that queer kids get treated like theological debris. They are mad that pulpits turn into political launchpads and then act offended when someone notices the missile smoke. They are mad that this book, whatever beauty or poetry or moral reflection it may contain, keeps getting hauled into court, into war, into classrooms, into bedrooms, into ballots, and used as a cudgel.

And some of us are mad for a more intimate reason.

Some of us know exactly what it costs to be shaped by a world where the Bible is not just read but obeyed like a surveillance device. Where every instinct has to be cross-examined. Where desire is suspect. Where certainty is virtue. Where your own conscience is treated like a hostile witness unless it agrees with the approved interpretation. That kind of life hollows people out. It makes them scared of their own interior. It teaches them to call fragmentation peace.

That is not a harmless private spirituality. That is formation through fear.

And once a person has been trained that way, they are easier to govern. Easier to mobilize. Easier to shame. Easier to turn against their neighbor. Easier to convince that cruelty is actually conviction. Easier to recruit into a worldview where domination feels like order and submission feels like virtue.

That is the piece people miss when they rush to defend the Bible like the text itself is what is under assault. The text is not bleeding. Institutions are. Narratives are. The authority of people who built their whole status on being believed without question is. The backlash is not against a book lying open on a table. It is against the machinery built around it. The machinery that says suffer quietly, obey completely, and call the damage sacred.

So no, people are not mad at the Bible.

They are mad at the empires, churches, politicians, and wounded little tyrants who keep using it as a permission slip to dominate other human beings and then acting shocked when someone finally says: enough.

Beaten by the Cross

I remember being 20 years old at Teen Mania’s Honor Academy, waking to the Braveheart soundtrack like Jesus needed his own boot camp trailer. Years later the same machinery feels like it’s running through American politics.

Beaten by the Cross

I remember being 20 years old at Teen Mania’s Honor Academy, getting ripped out of sleep to the Braveheart soundtrack like somebody thought Jesus needed his own boot camp trailer. It was theater, sure, but it was also conditioning. Adrenaline. Siege mentality. Holy-war pageantry for kids too young to know the difference between devotion and manipulation. I wasn’t watching it from the cheap seats either. I was in that world. I sat in meetings with Ron Luce. I was a raging bear. I saw the machine from the inside. And what chills me now is not just how intense it was back then. It’s how familiar it feels again. (1)

That same emotional architecture is all over America right now. Same trembling sense of chosen-ness. Same obsession with enemies. Same fantasy that the nation can be purified through domination if the right believers get enough power. The music changes. The branding gets slicker. The slogans get more political. But the engine is the same. Christian nationalism is not just churchy patriot cosplay. It is a theology of control wearing the flag like body armor. Researchers and watchdog groups have been warning for years that this movement fuses religious authority with state power, treats pluralism like weakness, and frames dissent as betrayal. It is not just about personal faith in public. It is about who gets to rule, who gets to be called righteous, and who gets crushed beneath the machinery. (2) (3)

And once you know that machinery from the inside, you start recognizing the smell of it before you even see the smoke. You see it when politicians talk like God personally deeded them the country. You hear it when pastors turn pulpits into war rooms. You feel it in the way empathy gets replaced by hierarchy, and conscience gets replaced by obedience. That world does not raise people to be free. It raises them to be useful. Useful to institutions. Useful to leaders. Useful to causes that demand your soul and then call the emptiness sanctification.

That is why this stuff matters to me in a way that is not abstract. I know what it is to be shaped by theology that does not just ask for belief but asks for surrender of your own interior life. I know what it does to the nervous system when fear and holiness get braided together until you cannot tell where one ends and the other begins. The growing literature on religious trauma is not making this up. Scholars and clinicians are increasingly naming the same pattern: authoritarian faith environments can produce chronic shame, hypervigilance, self-erasure, and long-term psychological harm. When your whole value system is wired around sin, submission, and cosmic threat, your body keeps the score even if your mouth still says amen. (4)

And the cruel genius of it all is that once people are formed that way, they are easier to mobilize politically. Easier to point. Easier to inflame. Easier to convince that cruelty is courage and domination is duty. That is why every time somebody tells me this movement is just about values, I want to laugh and throw a folding chair through a stained-glass window. Values do not need this much propaganda. Values do not need enemies this badly. Values do not need to terrorize children, erase dissent, and wrap raw political ambition in the voice of God. That is not virtue. That is empire with a worship band.

So when I say I remember being beaten by the cross, I do not mean some melodramatic metaphor cooked up for effect. I mean I remember what it feels like when a symbol that is supposed to stand for love gets turned into a disciplinary instrument. I remember what it feels like to have transcendence weaponized against your own humanity. And I remember enough to know this country is flirting with that same old fever dream again, only now it has microphones, campaign money, court seats, and millions of people who have been taught to mistake submission for righteousness.

That is the danger.

Not belief. Not spirituality. Not the search for meaning.

The danger is what happens when broken men grab the language of God like a club and start calling the bruises revival.

They Sell You a Shepherd

The whole scam works because people are trained to trust the robe, the stage, the certainty, the beard, the cadence, the smug little promise that somebody else has already heard from God for them.

They Sell You a Shepherd

They sell you a shepherd before you even know you’ve been taught to feel lost.

That’s the trick. That’s the bloodless little magic act at the center of so much religion. You are told, from the jump, that your instincts are compromised, your desires are suspect, your questions are dangerous, and your inner life is not to be trusted unless it has been run through the authorized filter. Then, once they’ve hollowed out your confidence, they step forward with a staff and a smile and offer to lead you home.

It is such a beautifully abusive design you almost have to admire the architecture of it. Almost.

The whole scam works because people are trained to trust the robe, the stage, the certainty, the beard, the cadence, the smug little promise that somebody else has already heard from God for them. It is dependency marketed as wisdom. Submission dressed up as peace. You are not encouraged to become. You are encouraged to comply. And every time you hand over your own moral center to a man who claims divine backing, you don’t become safer. You become governable.

This is not some abstract theological squabble for me. I know the world I’m talking about. I lived in it. I studied it. I knew the language well enough to disappear inside it. When a belief system teaches you that your own conscience is a liability unless it echoes approved doctrine, it does not produce freedom. It produces people who are easier to steer. Easier to shame. Easier to recruit into causes they would otherwise see clearly as cruel or absurd.

And that mechanism scales. It never stays private for long. Once enough people are trained to distrust themselves and outsource moral authority, all a political movement has to do is borrow the tone of the sanctuary and suddenly domination sounds sacred. That is one reason Christian nationalism works so well on people already marinated in authoritarian religion. The emotional habits are already there. Obey. Submit. Fear outsiders. Defend the righteous order. Distrust complexity. Long for strong leadership. Call it virtue. Call it God. Call it whatever keeps the machine fed.

But a shepherd can be a handler with better branding.

And a flock can be a population trained not to think.

That is why I have so little patience for the soft-focus language people use around spiritual authority. They act like leadership is inherently holy, like hierarchy is inherently wise, like being told what to think by someone who claims heaven’s endorsement is somehow safer than wrestling honestly with the mess of being human. It isn’t. It’s just tidier. It’s cleaner. It saves people from ambiguity. And some folks will trade away half their soul for the narcotic comfort of certainty.

But certainty is one of the favorite costumes cruelty wears.

That is the part too many people still do not want to admit.

They sell you a shepherd, and then one day you realize you were not being guided. You were being domesticated.

War Jesus

Somewhere along the line, the Prince of Peace got drafted into the war machine and handed a flag, a rifle, and a campaign donation link.

War Jesus

Somewhere along the line, the Prince of Peace got drafted into the war machine and handed a flag, a rifle, and a campaign donation link.

That is one hell of a trick when you think about it. Take a figure remembered for telling people to love enemies, bless persecutors, and stop killing each other, then run him through enough empire, enough fear, enough nationalism, enough pulpits frothing over power, and out comes a mascot for domination with a cross in one hand and a target list in the other. That version of Jesus is not holy. It is a ventriloquist dummy for violent people who need heaven to cosign their bloodlust.

And America has been very good at manufacturing that counterfeit Christ.

Not just in fringe corners either. In mainstream sermons. In campaign rhetoric. In military fetishism blessed from the stage. In the kind of political Christianity that talks constantly about righteousness but never seems to have a damn thing to say about humility, mercy, or the bodies left under the tank treads of history. The result is a theology where Jesus becomes less a teacher and more a tribal banner, less a moral challenge and more a divine permission structure for whatever the empire wants to do next.

That is how you get believers who can quote the Sermon on the Mount and still cheer for cruelty if it is aimed at the right enemies. That is how you get churches that preach salvation while casually blessing authoritarian politics. That is how you get a culture where faith is not measured by compassion but by aggression, not by conscience but by loyalty, not by truth but by whose side you are on when the missiles fly.

And once that happens, Jesus stops being a disruption to power and becomes one of its most useful employees.

The whole performance depends on forgetting the inconvenient parts. Forget the table-turning moral ferocity aimed at the pious and the powerful. Forget the warnings about wealth. Forget the solidarity with the poor. Forget the refusal to answer domination with domination. Forget the basic idea that if your religion makes you cruel, you have already missed the point. Instead, build a version of Jesus who speaks fluent nationalism, hates the same people you hate, and never gets in the way of your appetite for control. That is not theology. That is ventriloquism with better branding.

I know that machine because I have seen it from the inside. I know what it is like when spirituality gets fused with fear until every moral question becomes a loyalty test. I know how easy it is for people raised in authoritarian religion to slide from “God said” into “therefore nobody gets to question me.” And I know how dangerous that becomes when it gets plugged into a state, a movement, a media apparatus, and a public that has been taught to confuse violence with strength.

War Jesus is not the betrayal of one religion alone. It is a symptom of what happens every time human beings take the sacred, bleach out its moral challenge, and redeploy it in service of domination. It happened in empires. It happened in crusades. It happened in colonization. It is happening now whenever politicians and pastors wrap military aggression in divine language and call the smoke holy.

So no, I do not believe in War Jesus.

I believe he is one of the most grotesque idols this culture ever built.

A weaponized hallucination for people too addicted to power to admit they stopped following a teacher and started worshiping a mascot.
