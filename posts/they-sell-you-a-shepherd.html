They Sell You a Shepherd

They sell you a shepherd before you even know you’ve been taught to feel lost.

That’s the trick. That’s the bloodless little magic act at the center of so much religion. You are told, from the jump, that your instincts are compromised, your desires are suspect, your questions are dangerous, and your inner life is not to be trusted unless it has been run through the authorized filter. Then, once they’ve hollowed out your confidence, they step forward with a staff and a smile and offer to lead you home.

It is such a beautifully abusive design you almost have to admire the architecture of it. Almost.

The whole scam works because people are trained to trust the robe, the stage, the certainty, the beard, the cadence, the smug little promise that somebody else has already heard from God for them. It is dependency marketed as wisdom. Submission dressed up as peace. You are not encouraged to become. You are encouraged to comply. And every time you hand over your own moral center to a man who claims divine backing, you don’t become safer. You become governable.

This is not some abstract theological squabble for me. I know the world I’m talking about. I lived in it. I studied it. I knew the language well enough to disappear inside it. When a belief system teaches you that your own conscience is a liability unless it echoes approved doctrine, it does not produce freedom. It produces people who are easier to steer. Easier to shame. Easier to recruit into causes they would otherwise see clearly as cruel or absurd.

And that mechanism scales. It never stays private for long. Once enough people are trained to distrust themselves and outsource moral authority, all a political movement has to do is borrow the tone of the sanctuary and suddenly domination sounds sacred. That is one reason Christian nationalism works so well on people already marinated in authoritarian religion. The emotional habits are already there. Obey. Submit. Fear outsiders. Defend the righteous order. Distrust complexity. Long for strong leadership. Call it virtue. Call it God. Call it whatever keeps the machine fed.

But a shepherd can be a handler with better branding.

And a flock can be a population trained not to think.

That is why I have so little patience for the soft-focus language people use around spiritual authority. They act like leadership is inherently holy, like hierarchy is inherently wise, like being told what to think by someone who claims heaven’s endorsement is somehow safer than wrestling honestly with the mess of being human. It isn’t. It’s just tidier. It’s cleaner. It saves people from ambiguity. And some folks will trade away half their soul for the narcotic comfort of certainty.

But certainty is one of the favorite costumes cruelty wears.

That is the part too many people still do not want to admit.

They sell you a shepherd, and then one day you realize you were not being guided. You were being domesticated.
