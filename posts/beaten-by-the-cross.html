Beaten by the Cross

I remember being 20 years old at Teen Mania’s Honor Academy, getting ripped out of sleep to the Braveheart soundtrack like somebody thought Jesus needed his own boot camp trailer. It was theater, sure, but it was also conditioning. Adrenaline. Siege mentality. Holy-war pageantry for kids too young to know the difference between devotion and manipulation. I wasn’t watching it from the cheap seats either. I was in that world. I sat in meetings with Ron Luce. I was a raging bear. I saw the machine from the inside. And what chills me now is not just how intense it was back then. It’s how familiar it feels again. (1)

That same emotional architecture is all over America right now. Same trembling sense of chosen-ness. Same obsession with enemies. Same fantasy that the nation can be purified through domination if the right believers get enough power. The music changes. The branding gets slicker. The slogans get more political. But the engine is the same. Christian nationalism is not just churchy patriot cosplay. It is a theology of control wearing the flag like body armor. Researchers and watchdog groups have been warning for years that this movement fuses religious authority with state power, treats pluralism like weakness, and frames dissent as betrayal. It is not just about personal faith in public. It is about who gets to rule, who gets to be called righteous, and who gets crushed beneath the machinery. (2) (3)

And once you know that machinery from the inside, you start recognizing the smell of it before you even see the smoke. You see it when politicians talk like God personally deeded them the country. You hear it when pastors turn pulpits into war rooms. You feel it in the way empathy gets replaced by hierarchy, and conscience gets replaced by obedience. That world does not raise people to be free. It raises them to be useful. Useful to institutions. Useful to leaders. Useful to causes that demand your soul and then call the emptiness sanctification.

That is why this stuff matters to me in a way that is not abstract. I know what it is to be shaped by theology that does not just ask for belief but asks for surrender of your own interior life. I know what it does to the nervous system when fear and holiness get braided together until you cannot tell where one ends and the other begins. The growing literature on religious trauma is not making this up. Scholars and clinicians are increasingly naming the same pattern: authoritarian faith environments can produce chronic shame, hypervigilance, self-erasure, and long-term psychological harm. When your whole value system is wired around sin, submission, and cosmic threat, your body keeps the score even if your mouth still says amen. (4)

And the cruel genius of it all is that once people are formed that way, they are easier to mobilize politically. Easier to point. Easier to inflame. Easier to convince that cruelty is courage and domination is duty. That is why every time somebody tells me this movement is just about values, I want to laugh and throw a folding chair through a stained-glass window. Values do not need this much propaganda. Values do not need enemies this badly. Values do not need to terrorize children, erase dissent, and wrap raw political ambition in the voice of God. That is not virtue. That is empire with a worship band.

So when I say I remember being beaten by the cross, I do not mean some melodramatic metaphor cooked up for effect. I mean I remember what it feels like when a symbol that is supposed to stand for love gets turned into a disciplinary instrument. I remember what it feels like to have transcendence weaponized against your own humanity. And I remember enough to know this country is flirting with that same old fever dream again, only now it has microphones, campaign money, court seats, and millions of people who have been taught to mistake submission for righteousness.

That is the danger.

Not belief. Not spirituality. Not the search for meaning.

The danger is what happens when broken men grab the language of God like a club and start calling the bruises revival.
